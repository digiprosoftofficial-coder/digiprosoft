Hotel software is easy to over-buy and easy to under-buy. Large chains run enterprise property management systems with features a 30-room hotel will never touch; small properties often run on a register and a WhatsApp group. Somewhere in between is the set of features that a modern independent hotel, guest house or resort in Bangladesh genuinely needs. This guide lists them, in order of importance.

1. A live reservation calendar

Everything starts here. A visual calendar showing every room across the coming weeks, colour-coded by status — available, reserved, occupied, blocked — prevents the most expensive mistake in hospitality: the double booking. The calendar should support:

  • Different room types and rate plans (standard, deluxe, weekend, corporate)
  • Advance payments and booking sources (walk-in, phone, website, agent)
  • Group bookings that hold several rooms under one reservation
  • Drag-and-drop changes when a guest extends or moves rooms

2. Fast check-in and check-out

The front desk is where guests form their opinion of the hotel. Check-in should take a couple of minutes: confirm the booking, capture ID details, assign a room, print or send a registration card. Check-out should produce an accurate invoice in one click with every charge already on it. Anything slower creates queues and frustration.

3. Guest folios that capture every charge

A folio is the running bill for a stay. Room charges post automatically each night; restaurant orders, laundry, airport transfers and other services post when they happen. This matters because charges added from memory at check-out are the charges most often forgotten or disputed. The folio should handle taxes, service charges, discounts, split payments and company billing.

4. Housekeeping status

Reception needs to know which rooms are clean and ready — right now, not when housekeeping comes downstairs. Modern systems let housekeeping staff update room status (dirty, cleaning, clean, inspected, out of order) from a phone. Reception assigns rooms confidently and guests are not sent to rooms that are not ready.

5. Restaurant and point-of-sale integration

If the property has a restaurant, café or bar, orders should post to room folios directly. A separate restaurant system means re-entering charges, lost tickets and end-of-day reconciliation. An integrated POS, even a simple one, removes that friction and also handles walk-in customers who are not staying.

6. Reports management actually uses

A handful of reports cover most decisions:

  • Occupancy by day, week and month
  • Average daily rate (ADR) and revenue per available room
  • Revenue by source — which channels bring bookings
  • Outstanding balances — unpaid folios and company accounts
  • Night audit — a daily close that confirms all charges are posted

7. Roles and permissions

Receptionists, housekeeping, accounts and managers need different views. Receptionists should not be able to delete invoices; housekeeping should not see guest payment details. Permissions protect both the business and the staff.

8. Direct booking from your own website

Online travel agencies charge commission on every booking. A booking form on the hotel's own website that feeds directly into the reservation calendar turns some of those bookings into commission-free direct ones. It is one of the fastest ways hotel software pays for itself.

Features you can probably wait on

Revenue management algorithms, full channel managers connected to dozens of OTAs, loyalty programmes and mobile guest apps are valuable for larger operations but rarely justified for a property with fewer than 50 rooms in its first year of using software. Start with the core, then add.

How to choose

  1. Ask to see the reservation calendar with your own room types.
  2. Walk through a full stay: booking, check-in, restaurant charge, check-out.
  3. Ask how housekeeping updates room status.
  4. Ask which reports are available out of the box.
  5. Confirm training, support and what happens if the internet goes down.
